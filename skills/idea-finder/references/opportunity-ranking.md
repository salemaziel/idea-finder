# Canonical candidate assessment

This is the sole scoring and confidence reference. Assess the specific customer, incident and context, with a rationale and source for every dimension. Scores describe current evidence-informed attractiveness, not validation or a probability of success. Use integer 0–10 scores only when evidence supports a judgment; use **unknown** for missing evidence. No total, normalized subtotal or numeric ranking when any dimension is unknown. Once all five are assessed, a total out of 50 is optional and secondary to the reasons and tradeoffs. Never use a cutoff to eliminate or mandate pursuit.

| Dimension (10 points each) | Low anchor (0–3) | Middle anchor (4–7) | High anchor (8–10) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pain intensity | Observed minor inconvenience, little consequence | Specific disruption or material time/money cost | Severe documented loss, risk or impact; include magnitude and context |
| Frequency | Annual or rarer occurrence | Monthly to weekly | Daily or per transaction |
| Willingness to pay | Evidence buyer rejects spending or accepts status quo | Relevant existing spend or concrete budget/approval process | Economic commitment to address this exact problem; distinguish paid from promised |
| Founder fit | Demonstrated gaps in knowledge, interest or resources | Relevant experience with identifiable gaps | Deep knowledge, credibility, sustained interest and feasible resources |
| Accessibility | Demonstrated barriers to reaching affected users/buyers | Plausible channel or warm introductions | Specific reachable people and permission or established access |

Rare but costly problems remain eligible: a low frequency score does not cancel high consequences, avoidance spending or a reachable buyer. Do not assume a spreadsheet proves unmet demand or a friendly introduction proves willingness to pay. Do not turn hypothetical enthusiasm into a high WTP score. Explain judgment between anchors instead of suggesting numerical precision the evidence cannot support.

## Evidence and confidence (separate from score)

Label each item with source type, source/date, quote or observation, and scope:
- **Founder experience:** personal account; useful access and insight, not independent market confirmation.
- **Customer report:** specific firsthand account from a customer; distinguish reported behavior and prompted statements from observation.
- **Observed behavior:** directly seen workflow/artifact/action; state who observed it and what it actually proves.
- **External research:** identifiable source, excerpt/link/date; note representativeness and independence.
- **Inference:** interpretation, assumption or estimate; never rewrite as a quote or fact.

Attach confidence to claims/dimensions and summarize material gaps at candidate level:
- **Low:** assumptions, indirect evidence or unverified generalizations.
- **Medium:** specific firsthand reports, with bounded scope. One person's certainty does not establish prevalence.
- **High:** independent reports converge with behavioral or economic evidence for the same claim. Repeated copies of one report are not independent; high pain confidence need not mean high WTP confidence.

For every candidate record: specific persona/pain/workaround; five dimensions and their evidence; confidence and rationale; supporting evidence; contradictions; unresolved assumptions; next discriminating test. Preserve negative findings and previous assessments. Label old unexplained scores “legacy assessment — rubric/evidence unknown”; do not translate them into the new rubric without evidence.

## Founder goal overlay

For a small business, discuss attainable income, delivery effort, margins, manageable reach and founder constraints. For venture goals, separately investigate expansion potential, scale, growth channels, capital needs and defensibility. These are contextual questions, not extra common-score points or universal thresholds. Keep the common scores the same for identical evidence; let the goal change the recommendation and next test. Undecided founders may compare both paths without being pushed into venture ambitions.
